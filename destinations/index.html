The Timeless Heart of Sri Lanka

Anuradhapura

Discover Sri Lanka’s first capital and the sacred heart of its heritage. From ancient Buddhist temples to massive stupas and the world’s oldest sacred tree, Anuradhapura blends history, spirituality, and stunning ruins. A UNESCO World Heritage Site and part of the Cultural Triangle, this city is a must-visit for culture lovers and curious travelers alike.

Secrets Worth Discovering

Hidden gems

  • Sri Lanka’s most intricate ancient stone carving.
  • Rare relics from Anuradhapura’s golden era.
  • The finest guard stone of the ancient world.
  • Ingenious ancient bathing pools.
  • A serene statue of perfect meditation.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: It is a year-round destination. March to July is the hottest season. June is the busiest month.

Where to shop: Near the ancient sites, you’ll find small vendors selling Buddha statues, souvenirs, fresh fruit, corn on the cob, and other local treats—perfect for a quick bite or a keepsake. In town, you’ll have access to pharmacies, general stores, and banks, making it easy to stock up on essentials during your visit.

Where to eat: While Anuradhapura isn’t known for fine dining outside of hotels, you’ll still find some great local gems. Try Sanctuary at Tissawewa for a serene, heritage-inspired meal, enjoy casual bites at LuLu’s Feeding Point, relax by the water at The Lakeside, or grab something cozy and tasty at Banana Cafe.

Where to stay: Uga · Forest Rock

Sri Lanka’s Surf & Chill Paradise

Arugam Bay

Welcome to Arugam Bay, the surf capital of Sri Lanka and one of the top 10 surf spots in the world. Tucked away on the island’s southeast coast, this laid-back beach town offers epic waves, golden sands, and a vibrant nightlife scene. Beyond surfing, explore secluded beaches, lush lagoons, nearby national parks, and a touch of hidden history. Whether you’re chasing waves or just the perfect sunset, Arugam Bay is your ultimate beach escape.

Nature, history & adventure—just beyond the waves!

Hidden gems

  • Lahugala Park – Elephants & birdlife
  • Magul Viharaya – Ancient royal site
  • Okanda Devalaya – Sacred hilltop shrine
  • Crocodile Point – Crocs by the lake
  • Semanikulam Lake – Birds & buffalo views

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Surf season is from May to September. Suitable for off-season non-surf visits.

Where to shop: Whether you’re chasing waves or soaking up the sun, Arugam Bay has you covered with stylish swimwear, casual clothing, and surf gear. Stop by Apsara Bikini Tailor for custom beachwear, grab your board essentials at No Worries Surf Shop, or explore eco-friendly finds at Rice & Carry. It’s the perfect blend of local charm and coastal style.

Where to eat: From laid-back beach cafés to flavorful international cuisine, Arugam Bay serves up something for every taste. Top spots include Break Point and Hideaway Resort for chilled vibes, Why Not and the Roti Shop for quick bites, and Tandoori Hut for bold Indian flavors. For fresh, healthy options, check out Leaf and Vine, while Green Room, Gecko Café, and Beach Hut offer tasty meals with a view.

Where to stay: Jetwing Surf · Forest Rock · Bay Vista · Hideaway Resort

Beach Bliss Meets Bold Adventure

Bentota

Discover the jewel of Sri Lanka’s southwest coast. With golden beaches, stylish resorts, and a stunning lagoon teeming with life, Bentota is where relaxation meets excitement. Dive into water sports, cruise through the lush Madu River mangroves, or explore cinnamon islands and birdlife. Take in Sri Lankan design at its best with a visit to Geoffrey Bawa’s Lunuganga estate. As the sun sets, Bentota’s oceanfront nightlife comes alive—laid-back, vibrant, and unforgettable.

Beauty goes far beyond the beach.

Hidden gems

  • Dive into vibrant coral reefs and spot tropical fish
  • Fish the sea or river for a true local experience
  • Explore Cinnamon Island’s 100-year-old plantation
  • Visit Kande Vihare, home to a towering Buddha statue
  • Unwind at Moragalla Beach, rich with mangroves & water sports

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Bentota is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: From handcrafted souvenirs to precious gems, Bentota is full of shopping delights. Visit Ambalangoda for authentic traditional arts and crafts, or explore the Bentota Bazaar for colorful Batik textiles, handmade items, and local treasures. Just a short drive away, Meetiyagoda is world-renowned for its shimmering moonstones—a perfect keepsake from your coastal escape.

Where to eat: Bentota serves up a delicious mix of local and international flavors in stunning settings. Enjoy fresh seafood by the water at Diya Sisila or Pier 88 River Bar, indulge in authentic Sri Lankan cuisine at Randholee and Malli’s, or spice things up with Indian favorites at Bollywood Masala. For relaxed beachfront dining, try Rock Beach, Frangipani, Golden Grill, or Breeze—each offering a unique taste of Bentota’s vibrant culinary scene.

Where to stay: Cinnamon · Club Villa · Taj · Saman Villas

Where History Lives in Stone

Dambulla

Nestled among hills and lakes, Dambulla is a spiritual and cultural treasure. Its iconic Golden Buddha and ancient cave temples, dating back over 2,000 years, make it Sri Lanka’s best-preserved sacred site. Once a royal refuge, this UNESCO World Heritage Site offers a glimpse into a rich past—and it’s just a stone’s throw from the legendary Sigiriya Rock Fortress.

Step off the beaten path and discover Dambulla’s quiet wonders.

Hidden gems

  • Dambulla Economic Centre – Sri Lanka’s largest farmers' market
  • Pidurangala Rock – Epic Sigiriya views & ancient monastery
  • Menikdena Reserve – Ruins, nature trails & rare plants
  • Ederagala Cave Temple – A quiet stupa on the rocks

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Dambulla is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: Dambulla is all about fresh produce and local flavor. Visit the bustling Economic Centre for vibrant fruit and vegetable markets. While it’s not a hub for upscale shopping, the town offers an authentic taste of rural Sri Lankan life.

Where to eat: From luxury dining to local favorites, Dambulla has flavors for every palate. Enjoy gourmet meals at top hotels like Amaya Lake, Jetwing Lake, and Heritance Kandalama. For a more casual bite, head to local gems like The Orchid, Rithu, Sakura, Café Kanchan, Mango Mango, Athula, or the scenic Tree House at Kalundewa Retreat.

Where to stay: Kandalama · Kalundewa · Diyabubula.

Highlands, Hikes & Heavenly Views

Ella

In the misty highland of Sri Lanka is Ella, a lush green village with jaw-dropping views all around. Its landscape is dotted with mountains, valleys, and features Sri Lanka’s most famous railway line. The waterfalls of Ella are the most beautiful. On a clear night, the distant glow of the Great Basses lighthouse on the south coast is visible from Ella. And, at times, you can even see the Uda Walawe National Park. Situated in the tea country, with a 1000m elevation, its tea plantations in Ella thrive all around. Fresh mountain air and the mild climate make Ella a quiet scenic retreat for any weary raveler. There are plenty of mountain treks for a lovely walking holiday.

Ella’s best-kept secrets await beyond the trails.

Hidden gems

  • Flying Ravana Zipline – Soar 500m at 80kmph
  • Ella Wala Falls – Swim beneath a secluded jungle cascade
  • Ella Spice Garden – Discover thousands of local spices
  • Halpewatte Tea Factory – Watch tea-making come to life

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Ella is a year-round destination. September to December is the rainy season.

Where to shop: Ella may be a quaint village, but it’s a great spot to pick up premium Ceylon tea straight from the source. While you won’t find big-name boutiques, the charm lies in its local tea outlets and small shops offering authentic, highland-grown blends—perfect souvenirs from Sri Lanka’s hill country.

Where to eat: Ella is packed with cozy cafés and laid-back eateries perfect for refueling after a day of adventure. Sip and dine at the stylish Ceylon Tea Factory, unwind at the ever-popular Café Chill or Dream Café, take in panoramic views at Ella 360, or enjoy homestyle Sri Lankan flavors at the charming Matey Hut—all fantastic treats for the hungry traveler.

Where to stay: 98 Acres... · Nine Skies · Hide Ella... · The Pinnacle

Where History Meets the Sea

Galle

Step into Galle, Sri Lanka’s iconic coastal city, where colonial charm and ocean vibes collide. Explore the legendary Galle Fort, stroll past Dutch-era mansions, and savor the day’s fresh seafood straight from the harbor. With golden beaches, buzzing culture, and rich history, Galle is your gateway to a stylish seaside escape.

Uncover Galle’s quiet corners beyond the Fort walls.

Hidden gems

  • Japanese Peace Pagoda – Serenity atop Rumassala Hill
  • Historical Mansion Museum – Dutch-era treasures on display
  • St. Joseph’s Chapel – A blend of Portuguese and Dutch legacy
  • Maha Modara Hatchery – Saving sea turtles
  • Meeran Jumma Mosque – A Fort-front beauty facing Mecca
  • Galle Harbour – Ancient port still welcoming modern yachts

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Galle is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: Galle is a treasure trove for boutique lovers, especially around the iconic Fort. From handcrafted textiles to curated home décor and stylish apparel, the city blends heritage with modern flair. Browse standout stores like Laksana, Mimimango, Barefoot, Exotic Roots, and Raux Brothers for unique finds and thoughtful souvenirs.

Where to eat: Galle’s dining scene is as charming as its cobbled streets. Whether you’re craving gourmet fusion or authentic local flavors, there’s something to satisfy every palate. Enjoy culinary delights at The Fort Printers, Fortaleza, Poonie’s Kitchen, Gallery 52, Chambers, Dumplings, Pilgrim’s, Elita, and the vibrant Coconut Sambol—each offering a unique taste of this historic coastal city.

Misty Views & Tea-Laced Hills

Haputale

Perched on a narrow mountain ridge, Haputale offers breathtaking views, cool mountain air, and endless tea-covered slopes. This peaceful hill town is a scenic stopover on the way to Horton Plains, with train rides and winding drives that reveal the highlands’ hidden beauty. A blend of culture, nature, and serenity—Haputale is your highland escape.

Hidden gems

  • Surungamuni Shrine – A sacred cave steeped in ancient rituals
  • Dunhinda Falls – A misty cascade known for its smoky spray
  • Idalgashinna Railway Walk – A scenic trek through cloud forests
  • Diyaluma Falls Hike – Climb to epic views at Sri Lanka’s 2nd-highest waterfall
  • Ravana Falls & Cave – Legendary sites tied to the Ramayana epic

Top attractions

Best time to visit: It is an all-year destination.

Where to shop: In Haputale, the best souvenir is the region’s pride—Ceylon tea. Visit local tea factories or the Ceylon Tea Centre to pick up freshly processed leaves and blends straight from the source. It’s the perfect way to take home a taste of Sri Lanka’s misty highlands.

Where to eat: From cozy cafés to local favorites, Haputale has a variety of spots to satisfy your appetite. Enjoy fresh flavors at Lettuce and Cabbage, Sunrise, V Café, Glace Pastry and Bakery, or relax with a cup at the Golden Hill Tea Centre. Royal Top and Risara are also great picks, while many hotels offer delightful in-house dining experiences amidst stunning views.

Tea Country’s Hidden Gem

Hatton

Tucked in Sri Lanka’s highlands, Hatton is a dreamy escape wrapped in emerald tea fields, misty mornings, and colonial charm. From the spiritual climb to Adam’s Peak to scenic strolls by Castlereigh Reservoir, every view is postcard-perfect. With iconic estates like Bogawantalawa and Maskeliya, this is the heart of Ceylon tea country—serene, scenic, and steeped in legacy.

Uncover Hatton’s timeless charm beyond the tea trails.

Hidden gems

  • Devon Falls – A 318ft beauty nicknamed the Veil of the Valley
  • Rothschild & Norwood Estates – Centuries-old legends of Ceylon tea
  • Sri Bhakta Hanuman Temple – Home to Sri Lanka’s tallest Hanuman statue
  • Holy Trinity Church – Historic hilltop church once graced by Queen Elizabeth II

Top attractions

Best time to visit: It is a year-round destination. January to April is relatively dry and warmer.

Where to shop: Hatton is a tea lover’s dream—this is where you’ll find some of the finest Ceylon tea straight from the source. Visit local estates and tea centres to pick up premium blends. While the town isn’t known for broader retail therapy, save your other shopping indulgences for a more cosmopolitan stop.

Where to eat: From cozy hillside cafés to charming local diners, Hatton serves up a delightful mix of flavors. Top picks include Tea Hills, Railway Lodge, Zesta Tea Cup, and The Kiwis, offering everything from hearty local dishes to international bites. For scenic sips and satisfying meals, don’t miss Edinburgh Tea Train, Lake View Pub, or Hill Cool—each spot brings something special to the table.

Where to stay: Ceylon Tea Trails · Camellia Hills · Camellia Hills

A Culture Untouched

Jaffna

Tucked in Sri Lanka’s highlands, Hatton is a dreamy escape wrapped in emerald tea fields, misty mornings, and colonial charm. From the spiritual climb to Adam’s Peak to scenic strolls by Castlereigh Reservoir, every view is postcard-perfect. With iconic estates like Bogawantalawa and Maskeliya, this is the heart of Ceylon tea country—serene, scenic, and steeped in legacy.

Hidden gems

  • Jaffna Library – A striking example of regional architecture.
  • Casuarina Beach – White sands and shady casuarina trees.
  • Jaffna Market – Bursting with northern flavors and palmyra products.
  • Point Pedro – Sri Lanka’s northernmost tip, closest to India.
  • Delft Island – Home to wild horses, coral forts, and a giant Baobab.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Jaffna is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: While Jaffna may not have trendy boutiques, its vibrant markets and street vendors offer an authentic shopping experience. Browse for juicy Jaffna mangoes, traditional sweets, unique palmyra crafts, and an array of fresh local produce—true tastes of the north.

Where to eat: Jaffna’s food scene is a flavorful celebration of its rich Tamil heritage. Popular spots like Mangos, Malayan Café, Cosy Restaurant, Green Grass, Jaffna Heritage Hotel, and Hotel Rolex serve up local favourites—from spicy curries to dosas—making them must-visits for any culinary explorer.

The Cultural Heart of Sri Lanka

Kandy

Tucked in the central highlands, Kandy blends scenic charm with deep spirituality. Home to the sacred Temple of the Tooth and the vibrant Kandy Esala Perahera, this historic city pulses with Sinhala Buddhist heritage. Once the island’s final royal kingdom, Kandy is your gateway to Sri Lanka’s lush hill country and timeless traditions.

Hidden gems

  • Bahirawakanda Buddha Statue – An 88ft-tall icon overlooking the city.
  • Ceylon Tea Museum – Vintage brews and the story of Sri Lanka’s tea legacy.
  • National Railway Museum – A nostalgic ride through locomotive history.
  • Kandyan Dance & Drum Show – A vibrant cultural spectacle of rhythm and tradition.
  • Spice Garden – Discover aromatic treasures in their natural setting.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: It has favourable conditions all year. November to April is Kandy’s dry season.

Where to shop: Kandy is a treasure trove for authentic Sri Lankan crafts. Stroll through the city centre to find vibrant handlooms at Selyn, intricate batiks at Jayamali Batik Studio, unique art pieces at Rangala House Gallery, and timeless finds at Waruna Antiques. It’s the perfect place to pick up meaningful souvenirs with cultural charm.

Where to eat: From traditional flavors to modern fusion, Kandy’s culinary scene has something for every palate. Enjoy gourmet dining at Theva Cuisine or Kandy House, savor local favorites at the iconic Kandy Muslim Hotel and White House, or unwind with great food and views at Rockview and Slightly Chilled Lounge. Each offers a unique taste of this historic city.

Where to stay: The Elephant Stables · Ellerton Bungalow · Aarunya Nature

Sri Lanka’s Adventure Playground

Kitulgala

Famed as the setting for The Bridge on the River Kwai, Kitulgala is where nature and adrenaline collide. Just 60km from Kandy, this lush haven of rainforests, rivers, and waterfalls is Sri Lanka’s top spot for white-water rafting and jungle trekking. Whether you're chasing thrills or hidden trails, Kitulgala is your gateway to wild beauty and ancient history.

Where Nature Meets Adventure

Hidden gems

  • Soar above the treetops with thrilling forest zip-lining.
  • Discover the secluded beauty of Nalagana Ella Falls.
  • Try waterfall abseiling across Kitulgala’s cascading heights.
  • Tackle 7km of world-class rapids with white-water kayaking.
  • Go canyoning through slippery rocks into natural plunge pools.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: February to April is the driest season, while May to November is ideal for white water rafting.

Where to shop: Kitulgala isn’t your typical shopping destination—it’s all about the thrill of adventure and the beauty of nature. While you won’t find bustling markets or boutiques here, the experience more than makes up for it. Come for the rapids, waterfalls, and rain forest, not the retail therapy.

Where to eat: Dining in Kitulgala is all about relaxing in nature. Most hotels and adventure camps offer serene, open-air meals surrounded by lush greenery. For a satisfying sit-down, Kitulgala Rest House, The Plantation Hotel, and Heritage Family Restaurant are top picks—serving delicious fare with a side of scenic charm.

Where to stay: Palmstone · Moksha

Waves, Whales & Coconut Dreams

Mirissa

Nestled on Sri Lanka’s southern coast, Mirissa is where thrill meets chill. Cruise out for epic whale and dolphin encounters, catch perfect surf breaks, or simply lounge on its crescent beach fringed with swaying palms. As the sun sets, Mirissa’s vibrant nightlife comes alive—making every moment here pure seaside magic.

Beyond the Beach

Hidden gems

  • Dewundra Lighthouse – Visit Sri Lanka’s southernmost tip and climb one of its tallest lighthouses.
  • Mirissa Reef – Snorkel in crystal waters and spot friendly sea turtles.
  • Water Sports – Try your hand at jet skiing, wakeboarding, or tubing for an adrenaline rush.
  • Weligama Beach – Just 10 minutes away, it’s a surfer’s paradise for all skill levels.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Mirissa is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: Mirissa may be laid-back, but it has its own style. Sip local coffee at Podi Kade while browsing handmade souvenirs, pick up dazzling Sri Lankan gems at Roosara, grab chic swimwear at Lazy Ride, or gear up with branded surf equipment from Dylan’s—perfect keepsakes from the coast.

Where to eat: From sizzling seafood to fusion bites, Mirissa’s dining scene is full of flavour. Top picks include Zephyr for beachside vibes, Margherita Italiano for authentic pizza, Little Tuna for sushi lovers, and Wood Space and Shady Lane for laid-back café charm. Don’t miss the local favourite Dewmini Roti Shop or sunset cocktails at Sunset Bar—each a perfect taste of Mirissa.

Where to stay: Lantern Boutique · Nisala Villas · The Beach House

Culture, Coast, and Local Colour

Top attractions

While hotels cater to every craving, standout spots include Mr. Burger, Tusker, Sea View, Port Beach Restaurant & Pub, Dolce Vita, Simply Strawberry, and the ever-popular Lords—serving everything from fresh seafood to international favourites.

Little England in the Hills

Nuwara Eliya

With misty mountains, endless tea plantations, and cool, crisp air, Nuwara Eliya feels like a highland escape from a bygone era. Once the favourite hill station of British colonials, it’s now a dreamy destination for scenic train rides, cascading waterfalls, tea tasting, boating, golfing, and invigorating hikes. At 2,000 m above sea level, this timeless retreat blends natural beauty with nostalgic charm.

Where Legends Flow & Misty Trails Begin

Hidden gems

  • Ramboda Falls – A majestic, multi-tiered waterfall wrapped in greenery.
  • Galway’s Land – A tiny park teeming with birds and calm forest trails.
  • Seetha Amman Kovil – A mythical Ramayana temple with ancient footprints.
  • Moon Plains – A stunning 360° lookout at the edge of “Mini World’s End.”
  • Pedro Tea Estate – A living tea factory from the colonial era.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Nuwara Eliya temperature range between 10 – 20 degrees and is an all-year destination. April is the local peak season.

Where to shop: Nuwara Eliya offers a charming mix of shopping experiences. Browse woollens at the famous Bale Bazaar, pick up homemade preserves at the Franciscan Sister’s Jam Room, or explore the Grand Hotel Shopping Gallery for unique finds. Don’t miss the tea collections at Mackwoods Tea Centre or the sweet indulgences at the Strawberry Farm. For a touch of local craftsmanship, stop by the Jessica Factory.

Where to eat: From elegant hotel dining to cozy local favourites, Nuwara Eliya serves up a feast for every palate. Enjoy authentic flavours at Grand Indian and Grand Thai, savour Italian at Salmiya, or try the inviting dishes at Queenswood and Indian Summer. Whether you’re after comfort food or a fine dining experience, the city’s culinary scene won’t disappoint.

Where to stay: Stafford Bungalow · Stafford Bungalow · Jet. Warwick Gardens

Echoes of an Ancient Kingdom

Polonnaruwa

Step into a majestic past at Polonnaruwa, Sri Lanka’s medieval capital. Home to awe-inspiring ruins, colossal stone monuments, ancient temples, and royal reservoirs, this UNESCO World Heritage Site tells stories of Sinhala kings and Chola invaders. Wander through centuries-old marvels nestled in one of the country’s most fertile farming regions.

Uncover the Secrets of Sri Lanka’s Ancient Capital

Hidden gems

  • Pothgul Vihara – A mysterious structure believed to have housed sacred scriptures.
  • Royal Palace Complex – Explore the grand ruins of King Parakramabahu I’s palace and Audience Hall.
  • Shiva Devalaya 2 – The oldest Chola-built Hindu temple in the city, dating to the 10th century.
  • Gal Potha – Sri Lanka’s longest stone inscription, shaped like an ancient palm-leaf manuscript.
  • Archaeological Museum – A curated journey through artifacts and reconstructions of Polonnaruwa’s past.
  • King’s Council Chamber – Features stone pillars etched with the names of royal ministers.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Polonnaruwa is a year-round destination. October to December is the rainy season.

Where to shop: Polonnaruwa is more about ancient wonders than retail therapy. While you’ll find essentials and local snacks in town, upscale shopping is limited—so save the splurging for your next stop.

Where to eat: Tuck into authentic Sri Lankan flavours at Polonnaruwa’s local favourites. Jaga Food, Gami Gedara, Sudu Araliya, Thidas Arana, Wishma, Lake Restaurant, Govi Gedara, and Mahanuge serve hearty traditional dishes with warm hospitality.

Where to stay: The Deer Park · EKHO Lake House

The Lion Rock Fortress

Sigiriya

Often hailed as the Eighth Wonder of the World, Sigiriya is a 200-metre-high ancient rock fortress rising from Sri Lanka’s jungle heartland. Once a royal palace built by King Kashyapa in the 5th century, it dazzles with landscaped gardens, mirror walls, and legendary lion paws guarding the entrance. Climb to the summit for sweeping views and a walk through history at Sri Lanka’s most iconic UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Off the Beaten Path – Discover History, Hikes & Hidden Art

Hidden gems

  • Pidurangala Rock – A rewarding climb with stunning views of Sigiriya.
  • Pidurangala Vihara – A serene ancient temple nestled at the base of the rock.
  • Cobra Hood Cave – A natural rock formation shaped like a cobra’s hood.
  • Dakinigiriya Vihara – Tranquil ruins by a lake, perfect for peaceful exploration.
  • Sigiri Art Gallery – A vibrant stop for local art, masks, and handwoven textiles.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: October to December is Sigiriya’s wettest season. Early morning or late afternoon climbs are the best.

Where to shop: For a taste of local craftsmanship, head to Pathikada or the Sigiriya Craft Village for handmade treasures. Lanka Silks offers elegant textiles, while Vishwa Gems is the place to shop for Sri Lankan gemstones and jewellery.

Where to eat: Sigiriya serves up a rich mix of dining experiences. Enjoy fine dining at Heritance Kandalama, Water Garden, or Jetwing Vil Uyana, or savour authentic Sri Lankan dishes at popular local spots like Gamagedara, Ahinsa, Chooti, Nirvana, and Rasta Rant.

Where to stay: Water Garden · Jetwing Vil Uyana · Wild Grass

Surf, Stilt Fishers & Sandy Serenity

Weligama

Weligama, meaning “sandy village,” is a laid-back coastal gem on Sri Lanka’s southern shores. With its wide bay, perfect surf breaks, and iconic stilt fishermen, it’s a paradise for wave-chasers and culture lovers alike. Whether you're catching your first wave, lounging on untouched beaches, or indulging in fresh seafood, Weligama delivers the ultimate seaside escape—just minutes from Mirissa.

Beyond the Waves – Explore, Sip & Discover

Hidden gems

  • Handunugoda Tea Estate – Home to rare White Virgin Tea and 25 specialty lowland brews.
  • Ahangama Beach – A serene blend of surfable waves, sandy stretches, and rocky charm.
  • Martin Wickramasinghe Folk Museum – A cultural time capsule of Sri Lanka’s literary legend.
  • WaterMelon Surf Club – Learn to surf or gear up with friendly locals and quality rentals.
  • Dewundara Point – Whale and dolphin sightings await off this stunning southernmost cape.

Top attractions

Best time to visit: Weligama is a year-round destination.

Where to shop: Shopping in Weligama is low-key but charming. The Ceylon Sliders shop offers trendy clothing, accessories, and travel essentials. Along the roadside, you’ll find traditional lacework stalls, perfect for picking up locally made crafts. For broader shopping, Galle is just a short drive away.

Where to eat: Weligama is a food lover’s haven with a mix of laid-back beach vibes and flavorful cuisine. From fresh seafood at Fish Point and elevated dining at W15, to casual bites at Rotti Shop and Hangten Rooftop Restaurant, there’s something for every palate. Don’t miss the sweet treats at AVM Cream House, or the cozy charm of Kaori Café, Soul Café, Aloha Home and Kitchen, and Froggy — each adding their own unique flavour to the Weligama food scene.

Where to stay: Bay Marriott · Cape · Malabar Hill

Where the Wild Roam Free

Wilpattu National Park

Sri Lanka’s largest and oldest national park, Wilpattu is famed for its natural lakes, elusive leopards, and ancient wilderness. Just 30km from Anuradhapura, this vast reserve is a haven for elephants, sloth bears, and birds, all set against a backdrop of red earth and lush jungle. Hop in a safari jeep and uncover untamed beauty at every turn.

Untamed Wonders of Wilpattu

Top attractions

Best time to visit: The ideal time to visit Wilpattu National Park is during the dry season, from May to early September.

Where to shop: While Wilpattu National Park itself is all about nature and adventure, nearby Anuradhapura offers the best shopping options. Explore vibrant local markets for fresh fruits, vegetables, and herbal products, or pick up cultural handicrafts and souvenirs that reflect the region’s heritage. It’s the perfect stop to take a bit of Sri Lanka home with you.

Where to eat: Dining in Wilpattu is a unique experience, especially when you stay with luxury safari camp providers. Enjoy gourmet cuisine under the stars at camps like Mahoora, Leopard Trails, and Big Game Camp, where every meal is freshly prepared with local flavours and served in the heart of the wild. It’s a culinary adventure that complements your wildlife journey.

Where to stay: Leopard Trails · Glamping · The Backwaters Lodge

Where Wild Meets Ancient Wonder

Yala

Yala National Park is Sri Lanka’s top wildlife destination, famous for having one of the world’s highest leopard densities. Here, jungles meet the coastline, offering thrilling safaris with sightings of elephants, sloth bears, crocs, and peacocks. Beyond the wild, Yala hides ancient secrets—centuries-old stupas and reservoirs whisper stories of forgotten kingdoms.

Best time to visit: February to June is Yala’s dry season and has the most reliable wildlife sightings.

Where to shop: Yala is all about nature and adventure, so shopping options are minimal. Most hotels offer small boutiques with local handicrafts and souvenirs. For more variety, head to nearby towns for traditional items and artisan goods.

Where to eat: Dining in Yala blends luxury with wilderness charm. Many upscale hotels offer exceptional culinary experiences within their premises. For local flavors and casual dining, top picks include The Restaurant, The Chill, The Orchid, and Chef Lady Restaurant — each offering a unique taste of the region.

Where to stay: Kottawatta Village · Kottawatta River Bank Resort · Kele
